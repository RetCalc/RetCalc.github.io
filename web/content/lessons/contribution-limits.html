Every tax-advantaged account has a yearly cap on what can go in, and most have extra room once you turn 50. These are the figures for 2026. The IRS sets most of them each autumn for the following year, and the HSA's in the spring. They change most years, so check the year before relying on them.

401(k), 403(b), 457(b) and the TSP

A few rules decide how these apply to you:

  • The $24,500 is per person, not per plan. If you work two jobs in the same year, or change jobs, your contributions to both plans together can't go over it. Employers don't track each other's plans, so this one's up to you. Too much contributed has to be taken back out, with its earnings, by April 15 of the next year, or it's taxed twice.
  • A governmental 457(b) has its own separate limit. Someone with a 403(b) and a 457(b), common in public schools and hospitals, can put $24,500 into each. Some 457(b) plans also allow a special catch-up in the three years before the plan's normal retirement age, up to double the regular limit; you use it or the age-50 catch-up, whichever is larger, not both.
  • The catch-up depends on your age at the end of the year. Turn 50 in December and you can make the full catch-up for that year.
  • Employer contributions never use up your $24,500. They count only toward the $72,000.

The age 60–63 catch-up

Since 2025, people who turn 60, 61, 62 or 63 during the year can put in a larger catch-up: $11,250 for 2026 instead of $8,000. In the year you turn 64 it drops back to the regular catch-up. That gives four years of extra room, up to $35,750 per year in total, right before many people retire.

Plans don't have to offer it. The IRS has confirmed it's optional, so a plan can keep everyone at the regular $8,000. Check your plan's website or summary plan description.

The Roth catch-up rule for higher earners

From 2026, if your wages from your employer were more than $150,000 in 2025, any catch-up contributions you make to that employer's 401(k), 403(b) or governmental 457(b) must go in as Roth. The test uses the Social Security wages in box 3 of your 2025 W-2 from that employer, so pay from another job doesn't count toward it.

  • Your regular contributions, up to $24,500, can still be traditional. Only the catch-up portion has to be Roth.
  • If the plan doesn't offer a Roth option, people covered by the rule generally can't make catch-up contributions in it at all.
  • It doesn't apply to IRAs.
  • Plans are allowed to switch your catch-up to Roth automatically once your contributions pass $24,500, so you may not need to change anything; check how yours does it.

The law requiring this passed in 2022, but the IRS gave plans until the end of 2025 to get ready, so 2026 is the first year it applies. Final regulations formally take effect in 2027; for 2026, plans follow the law in good faith. If you're 50 or older and paid more than $150,000, check how your plan handles it. Traditional or Roth covers whether being pushed into Roth costs you anything; for many people in a high bracket it does, since the catch-up loses its deduction.

Other 2026 figures that affect saving

Why the figures move

Most limits rise with inflation, but only in whole steps: the 401(k) limit and its catch-ups move in $500 steps, the IRA limit in $500 steps, the IRA catch-up in $100 steps (it has only been indexed since 2024) and HSA limits in $50 steps. When inflation is low a limit can stay put, and after high inflation it can jump. The 60–63 catch-up is indexed on its own, which is why it stayed at $11,250 for 2026 while the regular catch-up rose to $8,000. The HSA's $1,000 catch-up and the $0–$10,000 ranges for married filing separately are fixed in law and never change.

Deadlines

  • 401(k), 403(b), 457(b) and SIMPLE contributions for 2026 must come out of pay received by December 31, 2026.
  • IRA and HSA contributions for 2026 can be made until April 15, 2027, the tax filing deadline, not counting extensions. Tell the provider which year each deposit is for.
  • Employer contributions can arrive later, up to the employer's own tax deadline.

Seeing it in your own plan

The Budget tool's Paycheck tab caps each saving line at its 2026 limit and shows the limit beside it: the 401(k) lines at $24,500 between them, the IRA at $7,500 and the HSA at the limit for your coverage. It doesn't add catch-up contributions, so at 50 or over you have more room than it shows. Where each dollar should go covers the order to fill them in.

Common questions

Can I fill a 401(k) and an IRA in the same year?

Yes. The limits are separate, so for 2026 that's $24,500 plus $7,500, or $32,000, before any catch-ups or HSA.

Does the employer match count toward my $24,500?

No. It counts only toward the $72,000 total for all contributions to the plan.

When are next year's limits announced?

Usually in late October or November, once the IRS has the inflation figures. HSA limits come out earlier, usually in the spring.
