Guardrails withdrawal calculator: spend more, cut when needed

Guardrails let you start spending more than the 4% rule allows, in exchange for agreeing to cut back when markets fall far enough. This page opens the Drawdown Simulator set up with Guyton-Klinger guardrails and runs them through every retirement since 1926, so you can see not just whether the money lasted, but how deep the cuts went.

How Guyton-Klinger guardrails work

Jonathan Guyton and William Klinger published the rules in 2006. Spending starts at a chosen rate and rises with inflation each year, like the 4% rule, until the withdrawal drifts too far from where it started as a share of the portfolio:

  • The upper guardrail: if this year's withdrawal has climbed more than 20% above the starting rate (from 5% to over 6%), spending is cut by 10%.
  • The lower guardrail: if it has fallen more than 20% below (under 4%), spending gets a 10% raise.
  • Optional rules: the paper also skipped the inflation raise after a losing year and stopped cutting in the final 15 years. Both are settings in the simulator, under Advanced.

A worked example

Pat retires with $1,000,000 in a 60/40 mix of US stocks and bonds and starts guardrails at 5%: $50,000 the first year, a quarter more than the 4% rule's $40,000. These are the numbers this page opens with.

  • It always lasted: the money lasted 30 years in all 70 historical retirements.
  • But everyone got cuts: every start cut spending at least once, and the typical start cut 4 times. 51 of the 70 spent less than year one's $50,000 at some point, and 37 less than $40,000, what the 4% rule would have paid every year.
  • The worst case: retiring in 1966, spending was cut 10 times, fell below $40,000 from year 9 and bottomed at $17,434 a year in today's dollars in year 17, a third of where it began.
  • The typical retiree spent more: across the middle start's 30 years, about $1.67 million in today's dollars, against $1.2 million for a steady $40,000.

Making the cuts livable

The deep cuts happen because each cut is a share of what you're spending, with no floor. Set a Minimum spending to see how the plan holds if you refuse to go below a level, and a Comfort line to count how often and how long spending dips under it. The Compare strategies view finds the most each strategy can spend while never going under your line.

Questions

How much more can guardrails spend than the 4% rule?

With a 60/40 mix over 30 years, starting at 5% lasted in every historical retirement, against 4% for a fixed withdrawal that never cuts. The price is spending that can fall a long way in a bad decade.

Can guardrails run out of money?

Rarely, because spending keeps falling as the portfolio does. That's why "the money lasted" is the wrong test for them: the useful question is how low spending went, which the simulator's scorecard and worst-year figures answer.

What are risk-based guardrails?

A newer version that moves spending when history's odds of the plan lasting leave a band, say 70% to 99%, then resets it to a target like 90%. It tends to make fewer, larger adjustments. It's one of the strategies in the simulator.

Should I skip inflation raises after a bad year?

It's one of Guyton and Klinger's rules and makes spending a little more conservative. Turn it on under Advanced to see the difference on your plan.
