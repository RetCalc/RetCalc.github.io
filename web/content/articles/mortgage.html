Mortgage calculator with taxes, insurance, PMI and extra payments

See the full monthly cost of a home, including principal, interest, property tax, insurance, PMI, HOA and upkeep, how the loan balance falls, and the amortization year by year. Then test extra payments, a lump-sum recast or a refinance.

How to use it

Enter the price, down payment, rate and length. Under 20% down adds PMI until you reach 20% equity. For a loan you already have, enter the current balance and years left, then turn on the options for extra payments and refinancing to see the interest saved and the break-even on closing costs.

A worked example

Morgan buys a $450,000 home with 20% down, $90,000, and borrows $360,000 for 30 years at 7.25%, the calculator’s starting rate.

  • Principal and interest: $2,456 per month. With property tax at 1.1% of the price ($413 per month) and $1,800 per year in insurance ($150), the housing payment is $3,018.
  • Total interest: $524,100 over 30 years, more than the amount borrowed. In the first years, most of each payment is interest; the balance falls slowly at first and quickly at the end.
  • Paying $200 per month extra: the loan is gone in 284 months, about 23 years and 8 months, and saves $130,583 of interest.
  • A 15-year loan at 6.5% instead: $3,136 per month, $680 more, but total interest of $204,478, less than 40% of the 30-year loan's.
  • With only 10% down: PMI at 0.6% of the loan adds $203 per month until the balance reaches 80% of the price, in month 104, $20,858 in all.

To try it, set Home price to $450,000, Down payment to 20% and Interest rate to 7.25% (where it starts). The calculator's rate starts at a rounded recent national average — it isn't updated week to week, so enter your own quote or today's average.

How to read your results

  • Monthly cost includes upkeep and utilities, which lenders ignore but you'll pay. Maintenance of about 1% of the price a year is a common rule of thumb.
  • The balance chart shows principal against interest over time.
  • The amortization table gives each year's interest, principal and closing balance.
  • For a refinance, the break-even month is when the monthly saving has repaid the closing costs. Staying past it means refinancing paid off.

Common mistakes

  • Budgeting only principal and interest. Tax, insurance, PMI, HOA and upkeep often add 30% or more.
  • Comparing loans by payment alone. A longer term lowers the payment and raises the total cost.
  • Refinancing without counting closing costs, or restarting a 30-year clock late in a loan.
  • Keeping PMI longer than needed. Ask the lender to remove it once you reach 20% equity.

Questions

How much house can I afford?

A common guideline keeps the house payment, including tax, insurance and PMI, under about 28% of gross income, and all debt payments under about 36%.

Is a 15-year or 30-year mortgage better?

A 15-year loan has a higher payment but usually a lower rate and far less total interest. A 30-year loan keeps the payment low and flexible; you can still pay extra.

When does PMI go away?

You can ask the lender to drop it at 20% equity, and it ends automatically at 22% based on the original schedule.

Should I pay extra on my mortgage or invest?

Extra payments earn exactly your mortgage rate, guaranteed. Investing may earn more over long periods but with risk. Many people pay down loans above about 6% and invest when the rate is lower, after getting any employer 401(k) match.

What is a mortgage recast?

After a large lump-sum payment, some lenders will re-figure your payment on the lower balance for a small fee, keeping the rate and end date. The payment drops; without a recast, it stays the same and the loan ends sooner.

When is refinancing worth it?

When you'll keep the loan past the break-even month, the point where the lower payment has repaid the closing costs. A rate drop of about 1 point or more often clears that bar within a few years.
