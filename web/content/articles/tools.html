Free financial planning calculators

Twelve tools for the money decisions around retirement and everyday life, each explained in plain language, with no sign-up and nothing stored on a server.

  • Retirement withdrawal calculator: will your money last?
  • Early retirement calculator: getting to 59½ without penalties, including 72(t) payments.
  • Roth conversion calculator and RMD calculator.
  • Healthcare costs before and after Medicare.
  • FIRE calculator, portfolio backtest, income tax, mortgage, rent vs. buy, college savings, budget and debt payoff.

Which tool answers your question

  • "Am I saving enough?" Start with the retirement calculator, or the readiness guide for a full check-up with a score.
  • "When could I stop working?" The FIRE calculator finds the age, and when you could stop saving altogether.
  • "How much can I spend once I retire?" The Drawdown Simulator tests a withdrawal rate against every retirement since 1926.
  • "I want to retire before 59½." The early retirement bridge compares every penalty-free route, and the 72(t) calculator sizes SEPP payments.
  • "Should I convert to a Roth?" The Roth conversion calculator projects your tax to 100 both ways; the RMD calculator shows the required withdrawals it could shrink.
  • "What will health insurance cost?" Healthcare costs covers ACA premiums before 65 and Medicare after.
  • "What will I take home?" Income tax for a paycheck or a year of retirement income, in all 50 states.
  • "Should I buy a home?" Rent vs. buy for the decision, then the mortgage calculator for the payment.
  • "How do I get out of debt?" Debt payoff compares avalanche and snowball.
  • "Where does my money go?" The budget, then college savings if there's a child to plan for.
  • "What do markets really do?" The portfolio backtest shows any stock and bond mix since 1926.

How the tools work together

Save Your household once, in the bar above every tool, and your ages, state, savings, income and retirement spending fill in across the calculators. Results can move between tools too: the Budget can take your take-home pay from Income Tax, and the bridge hands what's left at 59½ to the Drawdown Simulator.

What they have in common

  • Current rules: 2026 federal brackets, standard deductions, Social Security and Medicare figures, and each state's income tax.
  • Real market history: stock, bond and inflation data from 1926 through 2025, used to test plans against what actually happened.
  • Today's dollars: projections are shown in what money buys now, so they're easy to judge.
  • Private: every calculation runs in your browser. There's no account, and nothing you type is sent anywhere.
  • Checked: the math is covered by automated tests against IRS, SSA and CMS figures and independent calculations.

Questions

Are these calculators free?

Yes, all of them, with no sign-up, no ads and no limits.

Where should I start?

If you're not sure where you stand, the readiness guide asks about your money one question at a time and sends you to the tool that answers what you don't know.

Is my information saved?

Only in your own browser, if you save a scenario or your household. Nothing is sent to a server.
